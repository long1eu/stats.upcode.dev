Where today's web app still wins

First visit

4.2 MB against 3.2 MB; 32 s against 18 s on Fast 3G

Down from 4.8 MB this week: smaller fonts, and screens people open less often (estimates, invoices, settings) now download when first opened. Most of what is left is Flutter's drawing engine (1.5 MB) and the app itself. After the first visit, the browser keeps it all.

Invoice PDF

2.4 s against 1.3 s

The server answers equally fast to both. The PDF is two taps away in the new app. A one-tap button is ready and needs your OK (below).

Memory

111 MB against 61 MB

Mostly the fixed cost of Flutter's code and screen trees, not the company's data. It does not slow the app down on a normal computer.

Needs your OK: a PDF button on the invoice

A download button beside the invoice's ⋮ menu gets the PDF in one tap. Download PDF stays in the menu too. It shows on phones as well.

Needs your OK: the blinking dots

While an appointment is under way, a small dot next to it blinks once a second. It used to fade in and out. It now switches straight between its two shades. The fade kept the app redrawing about 20 times a second, even from screens that were not showing.

Before: the dot fades in over several frames
After: it switches in one frame Each picture is 0.6 s of the pill, one frame every 50 ms.

How this was measured

Chrome on a Mac mini, the two apps side by side against the same staging backend, signed in as the same test account. Each step is timed until the screen stops changing. "Fast 3G" is Chrome's built-in slow-network setting. A slower laptop or a busy network will make both apps slower; the comparison between them is what holds.

No appointment was under way on the test account during the runs, so the idle figures show both apps at rest.
